### Brand-history mismatch

The website's About Us page reads "Delivering Expert Service and Repairs for Over Ten Years." The recent Facebook and Instagram videos read "At Expert Services, we've dedicated 30+ years to serving Utah homes." Same brand, two tenures, three years apart in publication.

### Why this matters

Google's local algorithm uses NAP consistency as a primary trust signal — every directory carrying a different number reads as a separate confidence vote that the business identity may not be what it claims. Per-location call tracking is fine when each location's Google Business Profile and each location's Yelp listing carry the right per-location number — but here the social-profile numbers don't match any location page, and the Yelp listings carry numbers that don't appear anywhere on the website. Compounding the issue: a procurement-grade reader landing on the Facebook page sees "30+ years" and then visits the website seeing "over 10 years" — that's an unforced trust hit that costs deals before any conversation starts.

### Fixes — most doable this month

- **Pick one master phone per location and reconcile every directory.** Decide which call-tracker number is the canonical per-location number, then update Yelp, Facebook, Instagram, BBB, Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, and Bing Places to match the website's per-location page. The current 13-number sprawl can compress to 4-5 (one main + one per major location).
- **Reconcile the brand-history language.** Pick the true number (10, 20, or 30 years) and use it consistently across the website's About Us page, the Facebook About copy, the Instagram bio, and the LinkedIn company description. If the 30-year framing is the legacy of an earlier brand, name it in the About story ("Expert Services as it operates today is built on 30+ years of Utah plumbing experience from our team").
- **Claim BBB accreditation.** A 10-year (or 30-year) home-services brand with no BBB accreditation reads as either inattentive or non-credible to first-time-buyer audiences. Free profile claim is already done; accreditation adds the A+ tile to brand SERP.
- **Audit GBP secondary categories.** The Orem GBP primary category is already "Plumber" — appropriate for the highest-frequency consumer search. The work here is filling out the secondary categories (HVAC contractor, Electrician, Drain cleaning service, Water heater installation/repair service) so the listing competes on the full service breadth, not just plumbing. Same audit should apply to the SLC location and any future location listings.
- **Decide on callexpertservicesutah.com.** A second brand domain running its own Orem-targeted site fragments brand authority on local queries. Either 301-redirect to expertservicesutah.com/locations/orem/ or fold the content into the main site.

### On-page SEO — what's missing on expertservicesutah.com

- **No detectable JSON-LD schema.** A multi-location plumbing+HVAC+electrical brand should ship LocalBusiness schema with multi-location markup, Service schema for each service line, FAQPage schema on the FAQ page, and AggregateRating schema once the review-count question is reconciled. A two-paragraph "we just completed X at Y in [city]" block per page, refreshed monthly, is the cheapest schema-adjacent ranking lift.

- **AC Service Orem page ranks for a Spanish-intent query.** Best-ranking keyword is "ac repair spanish orem ut" (#7, vol 80). The page itself is in English. Either build a Spanish-language variant for Hispanic market in Orem (real opportunity) or update on-page copy to disambiguate the intent.
